Common Health Conditions Guide

=== FEVER ===

What is Fever?
Fever is a temporary increase in body temperature, often due to an illness. Normal body temperature is around 98.6°F (37°C). A fever is generally considered when body temperature rises above 100.4°F (38°C).

Common Causes:
- Viral infections (flu, cold)
- Bacterial infections
- Heat exhaustion
- Inflammatory conditions
- Medications or vaccinations

When to See a Doctor:
- Fever above 103°F (39.4°C)
- Fever lasting more than 3 days
- Severe headache or stiff neck
- Difficulty breathing
- Persistent vomiting
- Confusion or unusual behavior

Home Care:
- Rest and stay hydrated
- Take fever-reducing medication (acetaminophen or ibuprofen)
- Use light clothing and blankets
- Take lukewarm baths
- Monitor temperature regularly

=== HEADACHE ===

Types of Headaches:
1. Tension Headache: Most common, feels like a tight band around head
2. Migraine: Intense throbbing, often with nausea and light sensitivity
3. Sinus Headache: Associated with sinus pressure and congestion
4. Cluster Headache: Severe pain around one eye

Common Triggers:
- Stress and anxiety
- Dehydration
- Poor sleep
- Skipping meals
- Certain foods
- Eye strain

When to Seek Help:
- Sudden, severe headache ("worst headache of life")
- Headache with fever, stiff neck, confusion
- Headache after head injury
- Progressive worsening over days
- New type of headache after age 50

Relief Methods:
- Rest in a quiet, dark room
- Apply cold or hot compress
- Stay hydrated
- Over-the-counter pain relievers
- Regular sleep schedule
- Stress management

=== COMMON COLD ===

Symptoms:
- Runny or stuffy nose
- Sore throat
- Cough
- Mild body aches
- Sneezing
- Low-grade fever (sometimes)

Duration:
Usually lasts 7-10 days. Most symptoms improve after first few days.

Treatment:
- Get plenty of rest
- Drink lots of fluids (water, warm tea, soup)
- Use saline nasal drops
- Gargle with salt water for sore throat
- Over-the-counter cold medications for symptom relief

Prevention:
- Wash hands frequently
- Avoid touching face
- Stay away from sick people
- Keep surfaces clean
- Get adequate sleep
- Maintain healthy diet

When to See a Doctor:
- Symptoms lasting more than 10 days
- High fever (above 101.3°F)
- Severe headache or sinus pain
- Difficulty breathing or wheezing
- Symptoms that worsen after improving

=== STOMACH PAIN ===

Common Causes:
- Indigestion
- Gas and bloating
- Food poisoning
- Stomach flu (gastroenteritis)
- Constipation
- Stress

Warning Signs (Seek Immediate Care):
- Severe, sudden abdominal pain
- Pain with fever
- Bloody vomit or stool
- Persistent vomiting
- Swollen or tender abdomen
- Yellowing of skin or eyes

General Care:
- Eat bland foods (rice, bananas, toast)
- Stay hydrated with clear fluids
- Avoid spicy, fatty, or acidic foods
- Rest and avoid strenuous activity
- Use heating pad for comfort

=== ALLERGIES ===

Common Symptoms:
- Sneezing and runny nose
- Itchy, watery eyes
- Skin rash or hives
- Swelling
- Congestion

Common Allergens:
- Pollen (seasonal allergies)
- Dust mites
- Pet dander
- Certain foods
- Insect stings
- Medications

Management:
- Identify and avoid triggers
- Keep windows closed during high pollen days
- Use air purifiers
- Take antihistamines as directed
- Consider allergy testing

Severe Allergic Reaction (Anaphylaxis):
Emergency symptoms requiring immediate care:
- Difficulty breathing
- Swelling of throat or tongue
- Rapid pulse
- Dizziness or fainting
- Severe skin rash
Call emergency services immediately!

=== SKIN RASH ===

Common Causes:
- Contact dermatitis (reaction to substance)
- Eczema (dry, itchy skin)
- Heat rash
- Allergic reaction
- Infections

When to See a Doctor:
- Rash covers large area of body
- Accompanied by fever
- Blisters or oozing
- Severe pain or discomfort
- Signs of infection
- Doesn't improve after a week

General Care:
- Keep area clean and dry
- Avoid scratching
- Use gentle, fragrance-free products
- Apply moisturizer
- Take cool baths
- Avoid known irritants

IMPORTANT NOTE:
This information is for educational purposes only. Always consult with a healthcare professional for proper diagnosis and treatment. If you're experiencing a medical emergency, call emergency services immediately.

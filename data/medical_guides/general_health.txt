General Health and Wellness Guide

=== MAINTAINING GOOD HEALTH ===

Basic Health Principles:

1. Regular Exercise:
   - At least 150 minutes moderate activity per week
   - Or 75 minutes vigorous activity per week
   - Include strength training twice weekly
   - Simple activities: walking, swimming, cycling
   - Benefits: heart health, weight management, mood improvement

2. Balanced Nutrition:
   - Eat variety of fruits and vegetables
   - Choose whole grains
   - Include lean proteins
   - Limit processed foods and added sugars
   - Stay hydrated (8 glasses of water daily)
   - Moderate portion sizes

3. Quality Sleep:
   - Adults need 7-9 hours nightly
   - Maintain consistent sleep schedule
   - Create dark, quiet sleeping environment
   - Avoid screens before bedtime
   - Limit caffeine in afternoon/evening

4. Stress Management:
   - Practice relaxation techniques
   - Regular physical activity
   - Maintain social connections
   - Set realistic goals
   - Take breaks when needed
   - Seek help if overwhelmed

5. Preventive Care:
   - Regular health checkups
   - Stay current with vaccinations
   - Age-appropriate health screenings
   - Dental checkups twice yearly
   - Vision exams as recommended

=== COMMON HEALTH SCREENINGS ===

Blood Pressure:
- Check at least once every 2 years if normal
- More frequently if elevated
- High blood pressure often has no symptoms
- Important for heart health

Cholesterol:
- First check between ages 20-35
- Every 5 years if normal
- More often if high or risk factors present
- Managed through diet and sometimes medication

Diabetes Screening:
- Start at age 35, or earlier with risk factors
- Every 3 years if normal
- More frequently if prediabetic
- Simple blood test

Cancer Screenings (Based on Age and Gender):
- Discuss with doctor based on:
  * Age
  * Family history
  * Personal risk factors
  * Symptoms

=== CHRONIC CONDITION MANAGEMENT ===

High Blood Pressure (Hypertension):

Understanding Blood Pressure:
- Systolic (top number): pressure when heart beats
- Diastolic (bottom number): pressure between beats
- Normal: Less than 120/80 mmHg
- Elevated: 120-129/less than 80
- High: 130/80 or higher

Management:
- Take medications as prescribed
- Monitor blood pressure at home
- Reduce sodium intake
- Maintain healthy weight
- Regular exercise
- Limit alcohol
- Manage stress
- Regular doctor visits

Type 2 Diabetes:

Understanding Diabetes:
- Body doesn't properly use insulin
- Results in high blood sugar
- Can lead to serious complications if unmanaged

Management:
- Monitor blood sugar as directed
- Take medications consistently
- Follow meal plan
- Regular physical activity
- Maintain healthy weight
- Regular foot and eye exams
- Know symptoms of high/low blood sugar

Warning Signs:
- Increased thirst and urination
- Fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing wounds
- Tingling in hands or feet

=== MEDICATION MANAGEMENT ===

Taking Medications Safely:

Do's:
- Take exactly as prescribed
- Take at same times daily
- Don't skip doses
- Finish full course of antibiotics
- Store properly
- Check expiration dates
- Keep updated medication list

Don'ts:
- Don't share prescriptions
- Don't change dose without doctor approval
- Don't mix with alcohol without asking
- Don't stop suddenly without consulting doctor
- Don't take expired medications

Important Information:
- Know name, dose, and purpose of each medication
- Understand possible side effects
- Know what to do if you miss a dose
- Ask about food or drug interactions
- Report side effects to doctor

=== MENTAL HEALTH ===

Signs to Watch For:
- Persistent sadness or anxiety
- Significant sleep changes
- Loss of interest in activities
- Difficulty concentrating
- Changes in appetite or weight
- Feelings of hopelessness
- Withdrawing from others

When to Seek Help:
- Symptoms interfering with daily life
- Thoughts of self-harm
- Unable to cope with stress
- Substance use concerns
- Major life changes causing distress

Mental Health is Health:
- Just as important as physical health
- Many effective treatments available
- Seeking help is sign of strength
- Recovery is possible

=== EMERGENCY PREPAREDNESS ===

Know When to Call Emergency Services:

Immediate Medical Attention Needed:
- Chest pain or pressure
- Difficulty breathing
- Sudden severe headache
- Weakness or numbness on one side
- Severe bleeding
- Loss of consciousness
- Severe allergic reaction
- Suicidal thoughts

Heart Attack Warning Signs:
- Chest discomfort (pressure, squeezing, fullness)
- Upper body discomfort (arms, back, neck, jaw, stomach)
- Shortness of breath
- Cold sweat, nausea, lightheadedness

Stroke Warning Signs (Act FAST):
- Face drooping on one side
- Arm weakness or numbness
- Speech difficulty or slurred
- Time to call emergency services immediately

Emergency Information to Keep Handy:
- Emergency contact numbers
- List of current medications
- Known allergies
- Medical conditions
- Primary doctor contact
- Insurance information

=== HEALTHY LIFESTYLE HABITS ===

Daily Habits for Better Health:

Morning Routine:
- Drink water upon waking
- Eat nutritious breakfast
- Take morning medications if prescribed
- Light stretching or exercise

Throughout the Day:
- Stay hydrated
- Take movement breaks if sitting long periods
- Choose healthy snacks
- Practice good posture
- Wash hands frequently

Evening Routine:
- Wind down before bed
- Prepare for next day
- Reflect on day
- Get adequate sleep

Weekly Goals:
- Meal planning for healthy eating
- Schedule exercise activities
- Plan preventive care appointments
- Review and refill medications
- Social connections with friends/family

=== PREVENTIVE MEASURES ===

Infection Prevention:
- Wash hands regularly (20 seconds with soap)
- Cover coughs and sneezes
- Avoid touching face
- Stay home when sick
- Keep vaccinations current
- Clean frequently-touched surfaces

Injury Prevention:
- Use safety equipment (helmets, seatbelts)
- Keep home well-lit
- Remove tripping hazards
- Use handrails on stairs
- Wear appropriate footwear
- Practice safe food handling

=== HEALTH RESOURCES ===

When to Contact Healthcare Provider:
- New or worsening symptoms
- Medication questions or side effects
- Need for prescription refills
- Routine checkup scheduling
- Questions about test results
- Preventive care questions

Telehealth Options:
- Video or phone consultations
- Convenient for minor issues
- Follow-ups for chronic conditions
- Medication management
- Mental health services

Healthcare Team:
- Primary care physician (regular care)
- Specialists (specific conditions)
- Pharmacist (medication questions)
- Nurse practitioners (routine care)
- Mental health professionals

IMPORTANT NOTE:
This information is for general educational purposes only. It does not replace professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Always consult with qualified healthcare providers for personalized medical guidance. In case of emergency, call emergency services immediately.

Remember: Prevention is better than cure. Regular checkups, healthy lifestyle choices, and early intervention can help you maintain good health and prevent many serious conditions.
